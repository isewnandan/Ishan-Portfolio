Rotterdam Logistics
Performance Monitor

A data-driven analysis of the Port of Rotterdam's throughput performance from 2000 to 2023. Using official data from the Port of Rotterdam Authority, this project uncovers long-term trends, category shifts, and key logistics insights relevant to Dutch supply chain professionals.

Objective & Context

The Port of Rotterdam is the largest port in Europe and a critical hub for Dutch and international logistics. Understanding how throughput evolves over time — by volume and by category — is essential for supply chain decisions, port strategy, and infrastructure planning.

This analysis answers three questions: How has total throughput grown since 2000? Which cargo categories are gaining or losing market share? And what do these trends mean for the future of Rotterdam's logistics position?

// 02

Key Performance Indicators

Five headline KPIs derived from 24 years of official Port of Rotterdam throughput data.

// 03

Total Throughput Trend

Rotterdam processed 461.7 million tonnes in 2023, up 44% from 319.7 Mt in 2000. Growth was near-linear until 2015, after which the port plateaued around 460–469 Mt. Two clear disruptions are visible: the 2008–2009 financial crisis (−3.8%) and COVID-19 in 2020 (−6.5%), both followed by rapid recovery.

Total annual throughput in million tonnes, with key disruption events annotated. Peak of 469.4 Mt reached in 2019.

// 04

Category Breakdown & Growth

Not all cargo types grew equally. The stacked area chart reveals that containers grew significantly until 2015 before plateauing, while RoRo (Roll-on/Roll-off — trucks, cars, trailers) showed the most consistent and sustained growth of all categories. Liquid bulk remains the largest single category, while dry bulk has been in gradual decline.

Stacked area showing throughput by cargo category 2000–2023. Containers and RoRo gained share; dry bulk declined.

2023 throughput mix: Liquid Bulk dominates at 33%, followed by Containers at 32%.

Indexed growth (2000=100): RoRo +76%, Containers +92% — far outpacing total port growth.

// 05

Year-on-Year Volatility

Average annual growth is +1.66%, but the YoY chart reveals two major negative years. The port recovered from both disruptions within 1–2 years, demonstrating strong structural resilience. The post-2015 plateau suggests the port may be approaching capacity constraints.

Year-on-year throughput change (%). Green bars = growth, red = contraction. Dotted line shows long-run average of +1.66%.

// 06

Business Insights

Insight 01
RoRo is the fastest-growing category

RoRo throughput grew +76% since 2000, driven by European automotive trade and truck freight. This trend is structurally linked to EU single-market trade flows and resilient to economic cycles — making it a reliable growth driver for Rotterdam's future.

Insight 02
The port shows plateau behaviour post-2015

Since 2015, total throughput has hovered between 460–469 Mt with no clear upward trend. This capacity plateau may indicate infrastructure limits, modal competition, or market saturation — and warrants investment decisions in terminal expansion and digitisation.

Insight 03
Dry bulk is in structural decline

Dry bulk peaked in 2008 at 88.5 Mt and has declined to 74.2 Mt in 2023 (−16%). This reflects reduced coal imports as the Netherlands transitions to renewable energy — a direct consequence of the energy transition policy.

Insight 04
Rotterdam recovers fast from shocks

Both the 2009 financial crisis (−3.8%) and COVID-19 (−6.5%) were fully recovered within 1–2 years. Rotterdam's diversified cargo mix acts as a natural hedge — no single category is large enough to cause a structural collapse.

// 07

Methodology

01
Data collection

Throughput data sourced from Port of Rotterdam Authority annual statistics (2000–2023), covering 5 cargo categories: containers, liquid bulk, dry bulk, RoRo, and other breakbulk.

02
Data structuring

Data structured into a Pandas DataFrame with year-on-year growth calculations and indexed growth series (base year 2000 = 100) for comparative analysis.

03
Visualisation

Six charts built with Matplotlib and Seaborn: trend line, stacked area, YoY bar chart, pie chart, indexed growth comparison, and KPI panel. All styled consistently with the portfolio design system.

04
Interpretation

Insights derived by combining visual patterns with domain knowledge of Dutch logistics and port economics. Four business-relevant conclusions formulated for non-technical stakeholders.
